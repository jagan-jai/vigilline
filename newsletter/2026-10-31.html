FDA approves daraxonrasib (Rasonque), first-in-class RAS-targeted therapy for metastatic pancreatic cancer

The US regulator approved Rasonque (daraxonrasib) on 26 August 2026 for adults with metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma after prior treatment. Median overall survival was 13.2 months with the drug and 6.7 months with standard chemotherapy, with a hazard ratio for death of 0.40.

The US Food and Drug Administration approved Rasonque (daraxonrasib) on 26 August 2026 for adults with metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma who have received at least one prior systemic therapy or who are not candidates for multiagent systemic therapy. It is the first approval of a medicine from a new class of RAS(ON) multi-selective and mutant-selective inhibitors, which target multiple forms of the RAS protein, a key driver of tumour growth in most patients with pancreatic adenocarcinoma.

The approval is based on RASolute 302 (NCT06625320), a randomised, open-label, multicentre phase 3 trial of 500 adults with previously treated metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma and disease progression after one prior line of systemic therapy. In the overall population, median overall survival was 13.2 months (95% CI 10.0, not estimable) with Rasonque and 6.7 months (95% CI 5.8, 8.0) with investigator's choice of cytotoxic chemotherapy; the hazard ratio for death was 0.40 (95% CI 0.30, 0.53; p<0.0001). The trial also showed statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvements in progression-free survival, objective response rate and patient-reported outcomes.

Rasonque is a 300 mg tablet taken once daily and is now available in the US. The FDA granted Breakthrough Therapy and Orphan Drug designations, reviewed the application under Priority Review and the Commissioner's National Priority Voucher pilot programme, and made the approval effective on the date of its letter. Approval covers tumours with or without an identified RAS mutation and does not require a companion diagnostic test.

For competitive intelligence, the approved population is narrow but consequential: metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma after prior therapy, where options are limited. The company is advancing daraxonrasib in a global phase 3 programme in pancreatic cancer and metastatic RAS-mutant non-small cell lung cancer, where it remains investigational.
