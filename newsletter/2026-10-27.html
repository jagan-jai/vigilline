Merck and Kelun-Biotech ADC cuts risk of death by one-third in advanced endometrial cancer

The Phase 3 result, reported by STAT News during the ESMO congress in Madrid, is a mortality benefit of the size that a regulatory submission can rest on.

A Phase 3 trial of an antibody-drug conjugate developed by Merck and China’s Kelun-Biotech cut the risk of death by one-third in patients with advanced endometrial cancer, STAT News reported on Monday. The report appeared during the European Society for Medical Oncology congress in Madrid, where the week’s late-stage oncology data have been presented.

What makes the figure consequential is where it sits in the evidence hierarchy. Risk of death is the endpoint regulators and health-technology assessors weight most heavily, and a reduction of one-third is large enough that the trial’s design becomes the first question a competitive assessment has to answer: the comparator, the line of therapy and any biomarker selection all change the reading of the same number.

For a reader tracking endometrial cancer, the placement question is the commercial one. A survival benefit in first-line disease addresses a far larger population than one in the relapsed setting, and the two support different pricing and access arguments. The material reviewed does not specify the line of treatment, the comparator arm or the number of patients enrolled.

What follows is the filing. The material carries no submission date, no regulatory designation and no absolute survival figures, and VigilLine is not reporting numbers it cannot attribute. The presentation and any subsequent submission will settle whether the one-third describes an all-comer population or something narrower, and that answer decides how much of the indication the drug can address.

One death in Bristol Myers Squibb’s early CAR-T trial in autoimmune disease

Only 41% of women with abnormal breast screening in a KGMU study reached a diagnostic centre

Of 51,894 women screened in a KGMU surgical oncology study, about 1,557 had abnormal breast examination results, but only around 639 (41%) reached a diagnostic facility. Hospital experts said half of women in Uttar Pradesh are first tested when breast cancer is advanced.
